An attack drone designed to be cheap to build and simple to use.

HG-21, one-way attack unmanned aerial vehicle of Hyang Gu

At a glance How it is built

Simple enough to build in numbers.

The Fēngyǔ is Hyang Gu's domestically developed one-way attack UAV. It gives the country an affordable unmanned strike capability.

Unlike the reusable HG-12 Yǎnshí reconnaissance drone, it is intended for a single mission and is not expected to return.

It takes its inspiration from the growing role of first-person-view (FPV) drones in modern warfare. It favors affordability and straightforward manufacturing over the complexity of traditional military aircraft, so Hyang Gu can build a scalable platform in substantial quantities without straining the national defense budget.

Compact, light and made for one way.

Compact airframe

A small, lightweight airframe and an onboard control system support responsive flight. The design favors affordability, ease of maintenance before deployment and standardized production.

Operator's view

The FPV-style concept lets an operator watch a live visual feed during flight. This depends on the communications equipment being available and reliable.

No return trip

It isn't built for extended observation or repeated missions. Its airframe and equipment support a one-way role only.

Built to scale as resources allow.

Domestic production

The main goal is a production capability that grows as resources and manufacturing capacity permit. Standardized components and repeatable processes help control cost and simplify production.

Local expertise

The program builds home-grown skills in unmanned aviation, electronics, quality control and aircraft maintenance.

What it depends on

Long-term success rests on reliable component supplies, adequate testing and sustainable production funding.

Part of a larger program.

The Fēngyǔ complements conventional aircraft and reusable reconnaissance platforms. It doesn't replace them.
