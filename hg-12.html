It looks, records and comes home.

The Yǎnshí gives Hyang Gu's commanders a clearer picture of what is happening across the areas they monitor.

It favors sustained observation and reliability over speed. Where the HG-21 Fēngyǔ is a one-way attack UAV, the HG-12 is made to return from every mission and fly again, so routine surveillance doesn't depend on attack aircraft.

The airframe is standardized and the surveillance equipment is modular. That keeps maintenance cheap, training simple and domestic production realistic.

Built to stay up, not to rush.

A modest cruising speed and long endurance suit border watches, area surveys and routine observation. Its value comes from how long and how consistently it can observe.

How the Yǎnshí sees.

  • Electro-optical imaging

    A daylight camera records visible detail, helping personnel assess changes in monitored areas and document infrastructure conditions.

  • Thermal imaging

    An infrared imager picks out differences in surface temperature, which can help in darkness or low light.

  • Recording and transmission

    Imagery is recorded onboard and selected information is sent to an authorized station when communications are available. If the link drops, the recording remains.

  • Navigation and flight monitoring

    Onboard navigation and stabilization keep flight consistent and let the remote operator watch the aircraft's status. They support supervision; they don't replace it.

One aircraft, five jobs.

Modular equipment lets the same airframe take on different assignments.

Border and territorial surveillance

Long endurance lets it watch designated border regions and remote territory for far longer than a short observation flight. Recorded imagery helps authorities document changes and decide whether to look closer.

Battlefield reconnaissance

In exercises and fictional wartime scenarios it gives commanders a general view of terrain, visible activity and change. People still have to interpret the imagery, since a picture alone may not show identity, intent or exact capability.

Infrastructure monitoring

Roads, bridges, ports and power facilities can be imaged regularly to document damage, spot visible changes and decide which sites need inspection.

Search and rescue support

When equipped and authorized, it surveys wide areas and sends visual information to ground teams. Thermal imaging may help in suitable conditions, depending on the environment, equipment and search area.

Training and exercises

A reusable training fleet lets operators practice and refine standard procedures for collecting and reading aerial imagery, without spending one-way aircraft or crewed flight hours.

Connectivity can be interrupted, so mission planning and safety procedures have to allow for lost links, navigation uncertainty and equipment faults.

Made to be kept flying.

Standard parts

Replaceable components and common servicing procedures cut downtime between missions and let worn parts be swapped without scrapping the airframe.

Modular equipment

Approved equipment configurations can be adapted to different assignments without designing a new aircraft.

Built at home

Domestic production reduces reliance on foreign suppliers for routine repairs, as long as the electronics and other components can be sourced locally.

A lasting program also needs trained operators, maintenance staff, spare parts, inspection procedures, secure storage and quality control. A bigger production run doesn't automatically mean more aircraft ready for service.

Where it fits.

Keeping the roles apart lets Hyang Gu run routine surveillance without spending its attack UAVs.
